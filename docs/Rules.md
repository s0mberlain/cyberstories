# Cyberstories – Rules

A riddle game about real and realistic security incidents, for everyone in the company, with or without IT knowledge.

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Players | 3 to 12 people plus a game master |
| Duration | 5 to 15 minutes per card; a round of 3 to 5 cards fits into 45 minutes |
| Material | Cards with the riddle on the front and hints, solution and key takeaway on the back |
| Goal | Work out together what lies behind the incident, and learn from it |

## How to play

1. **Draw a card.** The game master draws a card, reads the title and the riddle aloud and places the card face down in front of them. Only the game master knows the solution.
2. **Ask questions.** The players ask questions, in turn or freely, that can be answered with **yes** or **no**. For example: “Was it an attack from outside?” or “Did an employee make a mistake?”
3. **Answer.** The game master only answers with:
   - **Yes** or **No**
   - **Irrelevant**, if the question doesn't matter for the solution
   - **Partly**, if the question is half right
4. **Give hints.** If the group is stuck for a few minutes, the game master reads out the first hint, later the second one.
5. **Solve.** The card is solved when the group has named the **core** of the solution: what happened and which mistake made it possible. Details such as years or technical terms don't need to be guessed.
6. **Reveal and discuss.** The game master reads out the full solution and the key takeaway. Then the group briefly discusses: Could this happen here too? What would we do?

The discussion in step 6 is the most important part of the game. It may take as long as the guessing.

## Difficulty levels

| Level | Suitable for |
|---|---|
| ■□□ easy | Getting started and mixed groups |
| ■■□ medium | Groups that have already played one card |
| ■■■ hard | Experienced groups or IT-savvy teams |

**Recommendation:** Start with an easy card so everyone understands the idea, then increase the difficulty. The web app filters cards by difficulty and by department.

## Tips for the game master

- **Read the solution beforehand.** If you know the story, you can answer confidently. When unsure, say “Irrelevant” or “Partly” rather than a wrong “No”.
- **Be generous.** If the group is close, say “Partly” or confirm what is already right.
- **Don't put anyone on the spot.** This is not about who knows IT. The mistakes in the stories are human, and it's fine to say so.
- **Include quieter participants.** In large groups, let people ask in turn.
- **Connect it to your own company.** After the key takeaway, briefly explain how things work in your company: Who do I call if I suspect something? Where do I hand in a USB stick I found?
- **Keep an eye on time.** After about 10 minutes without progress, give both hints and then reveal the solution.

## Competitive variant

For competitions between teams: every team plays the same card, and the game master counts the questions asked. The team with the fewest questions wins. Each hint used counts as five questions.
